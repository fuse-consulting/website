Why contact us

You’ll want us if…

When words are not enough, infographics are here to save the day. Each one is designed from the science, for the people who will read it.

Who we do this for
  • Scientists and research programs
  • Government agencies
  • Decision-makers
  • First Nations
  • Conservation organizations
  • Your report, article or funding application needs one strong figure to explain a concept, a process or a dataset.
  • Leadership, practitioners or the public need your findings without reading the full report.
  • You have more to say than one page allows, and less than a report.
  • You are reaching youth or a community group, and a formal document would lose them.
  • Your story unfolds across a landscape, and a map would carry it better than text.

Infographics aren’t just about pretty pictures. They are a strategic blend of art and science, designed to break the walls of the ivory tower.

Infographics have been shown to increase the impact and retention of science by keying into people’s natural desire for visual information. They are also associated with higher citation rates for scientific papers.

Format 2 of 5 · ½ to 3 pages

Infographic

A short, visually structured document that stands on its own. A half-page summary of a single study is often called a graphical abstract.

Choose it whenLeadership, practitioners or the public need your findings or recommendations without reading the full report.

Purpose
Summarize complex topics, research or information in a concise, accessible format.
Format
One to three pages, portrait, concise text and supporting visuals, ready to print or share as PDF or PNG.

Format 3 of 5 · 2 to 4 pages

Briefing Note

The same purpose as an infographic, with more text than graphics. Clients sometimes still call them infographics.

Choose it whenYou have more to convey than an infographic can hold, and paragraph-style text is needed.

Purpose
Summarize complex topics for decision-makers, practitioners, organizations or the public.
Format
Two to four pages, portrait, concise text and supporting visuals, ready to print or share as PDF or PNG.

Format 4 of 5 · a booklet

Zine

A small, informal illustrated booklet.

Choose it whenYou are reaching youth or community groups in an informal setting.

Purpose
Accessible, low-barrier science communication.
Format
A small print format, often A5 or smaller, printed as spreads like a standard booklet; illustrated, informal and creative in tone.

Format 5 of 5 · interactive

Story Map

An interactive, map-based digital narrative that combines text, images and geographic data (ArcGIS).

Choose it whenThe goal is public engagement or organizational storytelling. A geographic dimension is a strong fit, though not a requirement.

Purpose
Engage a broad audience with a story that unfolds across space.
Format
An interactive web application (ArcGIS StoryMaps), scroll-navigable, with maps, images, text and embedded media.
