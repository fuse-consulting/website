Knowledge Synthesis

Knowledge is scattered, siloed, inaccessible. We find it, organize it, and draw out what it means for management.

Layout only. This page keeps the structure and wording of the current site; the redesign concentrates on the home page and What We Do.

Our seven-step process

  1. Identify the objectivesWhat decision the synthesis has to serve.
  2. Define the audienceWho reads it, and what they already know.
  3. Target the knowledgeThe sources that matter, and the ones that don't.
  4. In-depth researchReading, sorting, weighing the evidence.
  5. Key messagesWhat the evidence says, in a handful of statements.
  6. Plain-language textWritten for the reader, not for the reviewer.
  7. Custom illustrationsFigures drawn for these messages, not borrowed.

Three products

Literature Review
A structured reading of the evidence on one question, with what it means for practice. Example: caribou habitat restoration.
Guidebook
A field-ready document that walks a practitioner through a method, step by step, with diagrams.
Best Management Practice
The recommended way to do one thing, with the evidence behind it. Example: pond levellers and culvert protection systems for beaver flow devices.
