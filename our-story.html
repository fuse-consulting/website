Our story

How we’ve grown into leaders in knowledge mobilization and conservation

Fuse is built on the belief that connecting research to practice is both possible and necessary

We want to see science change critical decisions! To achieve this, research needs to reach past academic journals and be embedded into our landscapes, schools, industries, and everyday lives.

We exist in this space between knowledge producers and knowledge users. For over a decade, we’ve been using science communication, visual storytelling, and facilitation to turn research into real-world action.

2007 → 2019

How we got here

  1. 2007-2012

    Looking for solutions

    Matthew Pyper realized that researchers produce knowledge that decision-makers rarely see or can’t use. His vision led him to join the Sustainable Forest Management Network, translating boreal forest research for government and industry practitioners across Canada. He then built a knowledge exchange program at the University of Alberta, synthesizing research into accessible formats. But the problem still needed a bigger scale solution.

  2. 2013

    Shelagh and Matthew create Fuse

    Most early work focused on sustainable development in the boreal forest. They produced a variety of written materials including briefing notes, knowledge syntheses, annual reports, and ecosystem-based management planning.

  3. 2017

    Art meets science

    Ecologist Sonya Odsen joins Fuse. A doodle of a bird in a notebook sparks an idea. What if visuals weren’t just decoration, but the medium itself? Fuse begins exploring the fusion of science and art as a core communication strategy.

  4. 2018

    A focus on facilitation

    Gatherings are fundamental to establishing connections across sectors and translating research into action. In recognition of this, both virtual and in-person event facilitation and management became core offerings at Fuse.

  5. 2019

    Visual science communication expands

    Kate Broadley joins the team, bringing deep expertise in infographics, data visualization, and custom illustrations. Fuse cements its reputation for science communication that’s as compelling to look at as it is credible.

Our approach

Listen, communicate, adapt

Good science communication is not a one-time lecture but involves a two-way dialogue that builds trust and sustains long-term relationships. To achieve this dialogue, we have learned to listen, communicate, and adapt.

Listen

Understand the needs of the target audience and look from their perspective. Is the message relevant and useful?

Communicate

Be concrete, concise, and credible. Do not overwhelm. Find the core of the message and present it in an accessible format.

Adapt

What worked and what didn’t? Which problems still need to be solved? Look for ways to further build and improve relationships.
